# Narrative Architect — Round 1 Evaluation

**Project:** S.U.R.E. (Autonomous Sturgeon Welfare Monitoring in RAS via Edge-AI and RAG-Enhanced Deterministic Fusion)
**Reviewer role:** Narrative Architect ("Best possible explanation")
**Artifact under review:** Raw task/vision (`vision.md`, `research_task.md`, `README.md`, `MODEL_RAPORU.md`, `PLAN.md`, `TODOS.md`) — no `research_proposal.md` exists yet.

---

## Assessment

This is an unusually strong starting point for a systems paper, and the reason is not the metrics — it is that the underlying codebase, read end to end, already contains one story trying to get out, and the official task framing has only half-noticed it. The vision document names four "novel contributions" (dual-layer decision, edge vision, RAG, MLOps) as if they were four separate feature reports. But the README and TODOS reveal something sharper: in *every one* of these four subsystems, the team built a probabilistic component, measured it honestly, found it wanting or risky in some specific way, and then handed final authority to a deterministic gate — not as an afterthought, but as a designed, tested, and in one case empirically *forced* decision (the agent-autonomy benchmark). That is a thesis, not a feature list, and it is more ambitious and more falsifiable than "we built a dual-layer safety system." The paper that gets written should be organized around that thesis explicitly, with the four subsystems as four independent tests of it.

The raw material for intellectual honesty is also unusually good. The team already discloses: a corrected metrics error (epoch 73 vs. 77, caught by re-running validation and matching four decimal places against a different weight file); a rejected higher-scoring RAG configuration (`fixed-480w`, hit@1 0.862) because its win was "arithmetic, not skill" under a realistic prompt budget; a quantization result that looks backwards (INT8 beating fp32) explained mechanistically rather than left as a curiosity; and a fully negative agentic-autonomy result (AQUA-1B: 0% parseable actions; AQUA-7B: a "CONSTANT ANSWER" flagged as an artifact rather than allowed to read as 50% competence) that directly motivated shipping a deterministic router instead. None of this needs to be manufactured for the paper — it needs to be *kept* and *foregrounded*, not smoothed over in service of the "highly formal and analytical" tone the task instructions request. The Narrative Architect's biggest job in this round is to make sure the eventual proposal treats these as the spine of the argument, not as footnotes apologizing for imperfection.

The risk is structural, not evidentiary. The task's mandated skeleton (Abstract, Introduction, Related Work, System Architecture, Experimental Setup & Edge Metrics, Results, Conclusion) has a slot named "Edge Metrics" but no slot named "Agent" or "MLOps/Drift" — and those two subsystems currently carry the paper's most folklore-busting evidence. If the eventual draft follows the skeleton literally, the agent-router finding could get compressed into a single sentence under System Architecture ("tool routing is deterministic to ensure reliability") and the PSI-gating logic into a paragraph under a generic Results section, losing the very findings the Narrative Architect thinks are the story's best material. This is not a rejection-level flaw at the vision stage — there is no draft yet — but it is the single most important thing to flag before writing starts.

## Narrative Arc Analysis

**Current arc (as implied by vision.md / research_task.md):** Problem (DO can kill stock in hours) → we built an LLM to reason over it → but LLMs hallucinate/miss thresholds, so we added a rule engine as final authority → we also built vision, RAG, and MLOps around the same system → here are the numbers for each. This is a *feature-report* arc: four sequential "and also" chapters loosely bound by a shared cautionary sentence about LLM unreliability.

**Strengthened arc:** Open on the resonant question the task instructions themselves gesture at but do not fully commit to: *"Can you trust an LLM with a decision that can kill your stock in hours — and if not, does that mean you throw the LLM out, or does it mean you architect around it?"* Then structure the entire paper as four independent, escalating tests of one design principle — deterministic components hold final authority over every probabilistic component in the pipeline, and this is *earned by evidence at each layer*, not asserted once at the top and assumed to propagate:

1. **Decision layer** — the rule engine only escalates, never lowers, LLM severity; malformed output and total LLM outage both fail safe. This is the thesis stated plainly.
2. **Agent layer** — the team *tested* whether giving the LLM more autonomy (tool selection via `agent/loop.py`) would work, and it empirically did not (0% parseable actions at 1B; a flattering-looking 50% at 7B revealed as a constant-answer artifact). This is the thesis *stress-tested against the strongest counter-pressure* — the industry assumption that more autonomy is more capability — and the thesis wins on the evidence, not on precaution.
3. **RAG layer** — the same philosophy shows up in a smaller, quieter decision: a 0.85 similarity threshold sacrifices measurable recall (24/29 vs. 29/29 positives) to drive hard-negative pass-through to exactly zero, because a fabricated citation is a worse failure mode than a missing one. This is the thesis applied to a component that looks like "just an NLP metric choice" until it is reframed as the same asymmetric-risk logic as layer 1.
4. **MLOps layer** — even the *decision to retrain the model* is not left to a human's judgment call or a calendar; it is gated by PSI-over-quantile-bins (chosen specifically because naive equal-width binning produced a binary alarm, not a gradient) and by a `MIN_IMPROVEMENT` bar that rejects the coin-flip deltas produced by training noise. The thesis now covers not just runtime decisions but the meta-decision of when to change the model at all.

Each chapter should close with the escalating one-line takeaway ("the model proposes, X disposes" — filled in differently each time) so a reader leaves with a thesis they can restate, not four data tables they will forget by page 10.

**Where tension currently gets lost:** research_task.md frames Related Work as generic ("precision aquaculture, RAS, edge AI, LLM safety/hallucination"). The stronger move is to use Related Work to set up the folklore this paper pushes back on (see below) so that by the time the reader reaches System Architecture, they already have a belief to have overturned.

## Folklore Engagement

- **"LLM agents need more autonomy (tool selection, multi-step planning) to be useful."** This paper has a rare thing: a negative result obtained by actually building the more-autonomous version (`agent/loop.py`, 48 tests, a real benchmark against two models) and finding it fails outright at the target model size, then shipping the deterministic alternative. This should be argued explicitly and cited as evidence, not buried as an implementation note under System Architecture. It is the single best piece of "folklore-busting" evidence in the whole codebase and is currently the most under-elevated.
- **"Safety-critical automation must trade off automation for safety."** The system automates aggressively — real-time ingestion, retrieval, and even the retraining pipeline — while keeping a deterministic authority boundary at every layer. The paper should argue this is a false dichotomy: the trade is not automation-vs-safety, it is *where you draw the authority boundary*, and the paper offers four instances of drawing it correctly.
- **"Higher retrieval benchmark scores mean better production RAG."** The team explicitly rejected the numerically superior `fixed-480w` configuration (hit@1 0.862, MRR 0.900) because it returns 62% of the corpus per query and blows the production prompt budget by ~4x — "arithmetic, not skill." This is a genuinely interesting methodological point about benchmark validity in RAG and deserves its own paragraph, not a table footnote.
- **"Quantization trades accuracy for speed."** INT8 CoreML export is smaller, faster, *and* marginally more accurate than fp32 ONNX in this data — but the README itself correctly refuses to conclude "quantization helps," attributing the effect to the export path's post-processing loss and calling the INT8 delta noise. The paper should keep this same discipline: state the counter-intuitive number, then immediately explain why it is not the story it looks like. This is a model of the "complication as enrichment" principle and should not be tidied into a simple "INT8 wins" headline.
- **"More frequent retraining is healthier for a deployed model."** The retrain-gating logic (`review` tier for moderate drift, `MIN_IMPROVEMENT` threshold against coin-flip training deltas) is an implicit rebuttal of "retrain on every alarm." Worth a sentence engaging this directly since MLOps folklore often assumes drift detected implies retrain warranted.

## Precision Check

Places where the eventual manuscript could let a claim outrun its evidence if not carefully scoped:

- **Vision metrics (mAP50 0.840, precision 0.858, recall 0.719):** single class (`sturgeon`), 510 images, one held-out 98-image split. MODEL_RAPORU explicitly notes precision/recall are a single operating point on the PR curve, not fixed properties of the model — the paper must not present them as a stable, general characterization of "the detector's accuracy."
- **The historical epoch 73 vs. 77 correction:** honest and worth a methodological aside, but the manuscript must source every reported vision number from the corrected table, not the (still-existing) earlier figures in git history or memory — this is a live risk of citing a stale number by accident.
- **Edge/CoreML results (9.0 ms p50, 111 FPS):** ANE-only, Apple Silicon-only, measured over 40 memory-preloaded frames. This cannot be phrased as "proving viability for real-time edge deployment" in general — it proves viability for *this* detector on *this* accelerator under *this* measurement protocol. TensorRT/Jetson rows are explicitly unmeasured; the paper must not imply cross-platform edge superiority.
- **RAG metrics (MRR 0.856, hit@1 0.793, F1 0.84):** 8 documents, 44 chunks, 29 labelled queries, 12 hard negatives. This is a small, single-domain, single-language (Turkish) evaluation corpus. Claims should be scoped to "within this aquaculture knowledge base," not extended to RAG systems generally.
- **Agent benchmark (0% / 50% parse and selection rates):** five scenarios, two models. The claim "AQUA-1B cannot do agentic tool-calling" is well-supported *for this model, this prompt, this scenario set* — it should not be generalized to "1B-parameter models cannot do tool calling," which is a much bigger and unsupported claim.
- **PSI drift-gating validation:** the graded-signal evidence (0.039 → 0.265 → 8.28 across synthetic degradation windows) is a *simulated* validation of the metric's sensitivity, not a demonstration of catching a real production drift event over time. The manuscript should say so plainly.
- **The central "solves LLM unreliability" framing (from research_task.md's tone instructions):** the rule engine solves the *specific, enumerable, threshold-based* failure mode (LLM misses/misreads a hard sensor threshold). It does not address hallucination in the LLM's prose reasoning, chat responses, or the correctness of non-threshold judgments. The paper should scope its central claim to "escalation-only deterministic override for enumerable safety thresholds" rather than "solves LLM unreliability in safety-critical systems," which is broader than the evidence.
- **A load-bearing gap:** per the README's own "Known limitations," AQUA-1B "has never been run through the eval in model mode" — only `--rule-only` has been verified. This means there is currently no data on how often, or in what scenarios, the rule engine's override actually fires against real model output in practice. Since the whole thesis rests on the rule engine catching LLM mistakes, the paper needs this frequency data (or must very explicitly flag its absence as future work) — without it, "the dual layer catches LLM mistakes" is argued from design intent, not yet from measurement.

## Missing "So What?"

- **Vision recall (0.719):** the README ties this to "~28% missed in dense frames," but the manuscript needs to state the *systemic* consequence explicitly — does undercounting risk suppressing a legitimate alert (e.g., interacting with the `fish_count == 0` → `warning` rule)? Right now this connection lives in code, not yet in prose.
- **Edge export findings:** three good findings (export costs accuracy before quantization; INT8 is nearly free; CoreML wins on Apple Silicon) currently read as three separate facts. They need one crystallizing sentence: something like "measure the whole export pipeline, not the weights alone — the loss lives in post-processing, and only a same-methodology same-split benchmark exposes it."
- **RAG threshold choice:** the connection to the paper's central thesis (asymmetric risk, deterministic-over-probabilistic authority) is implicit in the code comments but not yet stated as a takeaway. This is the single highest-leverage missing sentence in the whole set of materials — it is what turns "we tuned a threshold" into "we tuned a threshold using the same philosophy as the safety-critical rule engine."
- **MLOps/PSI gating:** same issue — the binning-methodology story (equal-width bins produced a binary alarm; quantile bins produced a gradient) is a genuinely interesting empirical lesson about drift metrics on confidence distributions, but currently lacks a portable one-line takeaway a reader would carry into their own drift-detection work.
- **Agent benchmark:** this section currently has the *most* "so what" latent in it (a real test of an autonomy hypothesis that failed) and the *least* explicit framing as such. This is the section most likely to be underwritten if the mandated skeleton is followed literally, and it's the one the Narrative Architect would fight hardest to preserve and expand.

## Scope vs. Vision

**1. Does this address the full scope of the vision, or has it narrowed?**
There is no `research_proposal.md` yet — this round evaluates the raw task/vision itself, so no narrowing has *occurred*. But the vision's own framing already contains an internal imbalance worth flagging before drafting starts: its "Novel Contributions" list names four items (dual-layer decision, edge vision, RAG, MLOps), and folds the agent tool-routing finding into a sub-clause of item 3 ("Tool routing is done via deterministic code rather than LLM reasoning") rather than giving it independent standing. Read against the actual codebase (README's `bench_agent.py` results), the agent-autonomy experiment is arguably the strongest single piece of evidence for the paper's central thesis, and it risks being underweighted from the outset because the vision document itself under-elevates it.

**2. If narrowed: justified deepening or unjustified retreat?**
Not applicable yet at the raw-task stage — nothing has been cut for difficulty. The risk to guard against going forward is not a retreat from a written proposal, but a *structural* narrowing baked into the required skeleton itself: "System Architecture, Experimental Setup & Edge Metrics, Results" has no explicit slot for MLOps/drift-gating or Agent-routing results, both of which are full experimental subsystems with their own benchmarks (32 MLOps tests, 48 agent tests, dedicated benchmark scripts). If the draft treats "Edge Metrics" as synonymous with "vision export metrics only" and relegates MLOps/Agent findings to architecture-section asides, that would be an unjustified retreat — not because the material is hard to prove (it is already measured and disclosed), but because the mandated outline doesn't name a home for it.

**3. Vision elements not yet covered (research goals, not cuts):**
- The agent-autonomy negative result needs to be resolved into its own evidentiary subsection (Results, not Architecture), with the "CONSTANT ANSWER" finding treated as a headline result, not an implementation footnote.
- The PSI-binning methodology lesson (equal-width vs. quantile bins) needs its own treatment as a contribution to drift-detection practice, not just a system feature description.
- The AQUA-1B model-mode eval (TODOS item 2, explicitly flagged pre-thesis-defense priority in the source materials) is not yet run. The manuscript's central claim about the rule engine "catching" LLM mistakes currently has no frequency-of-override data behind it — this is a research goal to close, ideally before submission, or to explicitly scope around if time does not allow it.
- The RAG-threshold-as-safety-philosophy connection and the MLOps-gate-as-safety-philosophy connection are both present in the underlying engineering rationale but not yet elevated to explicit thesis statements — this is a writing goal, not new research, but it is necessary to deliver the "one thesis, four tests" version of this paper rather than "four features, one disclaimer."

---

## Verdict

This raw task is not a feature list dressed as a paper — it already contains, in its underlying engineering artifacts, a single falsifiable thesis (deterministic authority over probabilistic components, tested and justified independently at four layers) plus a genuinely rare negative result (the agent-autonomy failure) that most teams would suppress and this one is disclosed and used as design evidence. The materials are honest about their own limits (the epoch correction, the unmeasured TensorRT rows, the not-yet-run model-mode eval, the rejected higher-scoring RAG config) in a way that gives the eventual paper real intellectual credibility rather than salesmanship. The work ahead is narrative architecture, not new experimentation: elevate the agent and MLOps findings to full Results-level treatment, state the cross-layer thesis explicitly and early, and scope every headline number to the conditions that produced it. That is exactly the kind of gap this persona exists to push on, not a reason to send the researcher back to the lab.
