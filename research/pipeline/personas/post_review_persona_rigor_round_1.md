## Assessment

This paper earns its 8/10. The specific defect the Phase-10 reviewer flagged — the Introduction's "weeks after the correction" line contradicting the "43 minutes" figure stated in Results and Known Limitations — has been fixed: a manuscript-wide grep for "weeks" and "weeks after" returns zero matches, and all three occurrences of the RAG-leak timeline (`introduction.tex:72`, `results.tex:350`, `known_limitations.tex:44`) now consistently state "43 minutes." That was the review's only hard must-fix action, and it is resolved cleanly with no collateral change to any numeric or scientific claim.

The paper's central discipline — stating $n=8$ as a census of a fixed scenario population rather than a statistically powered rate — holds everywhere it is invoked, not just in the sections a careless author would remember to hedge. I traced every occurrence of "n=8," "eight scenarios," "8/8," and "one in eight" across the manuscript (introduction, experimental_setup, results, system_architecture, known_limitations, appendix): each is either an explicit fraction-of-eight ("one case in eight," "50%... of the eight," "two of three") or is immediately paired with the disclaimer that no confidence interval is implied. The Abstract in particular is disciplined about this — it says "genuine correction occurs in only one case in eight," a phrasing that cannot be misread as a population rate the way a bare "12%" could. The one place a bare percentage appears without an adjacent n-tag is the opening of Discussion §7 ("turns out to describe only 12% of what actually happens"), but this sentence is explicitly restating a figure already scoped with $n=8$ two sections earlier in Results, not introducing a fresh claim — I do not read this as overreach, only as a very minor stylistic softness in an otherwise airtight scoping discipline. The Conclusion is equally careful, converting every percentage back into a count ("half of the eight exercised scenarios," "two of three") rather than restating percentages that could drift toward sounding general.

The two specific verification items this persona was asked to re-check both pass. First, the LoRA adapter caveat: `system_architecture.tex` states plainly, at first mention, that "this scope note applies strictly to results derived from this adapter (Section 5's decision-layer findings); it does not apply to the agentic tool-routing results reported in the same section, which use the base model with no adapter loaded at all," and `known_limitations.tex` repeats this exact scoping under its own paragraph heading ("The adapter-provenance caveat is scoped narrowly"), stating explicitly that misapplying it to EXP04 "would be a new factual error, not added precision." `experimental_setup.tex` independently confirms EXP04 ran "base weights, no LoRA adapter loaded for either" model, and `results.tex` §5.2 opens by restating that the caveat "does not apply to this subsection." This is scoped correctly in every location it appears, with no drift. Second, Related Work's novelty framing: §2.2 ("What this paper does not claim to have invented") is explicit and unhedged — it names IEC 61508/61511 Safety Instrumented Systems and RL-safety shielding by name, states "the honest answer... is no," and even goes further than the minimum bar by naming the one place its own independence claim is *weaker* than the industrial-safety literature it borrows from (logical vs. physical independence, `backend/rules.py` as a shared-import module rather than physically separate hardware). This matches `novelty_flags.json`'s own `EQUIVALENT_KNOWN` classification for exactly this claim (C1), and the paper does not quietly walk back that classification anywhere else in the text — §2.5 restates the same boundary a third time when defining "what is actually new here."

## Novelty Analysis (final check against the actual text)

Cross-referencing `novelty_flags.json` against the final text, every claim the adversarial lit-review flagged as `EQUIVALENT_KNOWN`, `KNOWN`, or `PARTIAL` is reformulated in the paper exactly as recommended, not merely acknowledged in passing:
- C1 (deterministic override itself): reformulated in §2.2, as discussed above.
- C8 (PSI quantile-vs-equal-width binning sensitivity): §2.4 states this is "already documented, standard practice in the credit-scoring literature PSI originates from," citing four sources, before stating the narrow actual contribution (applying it to a new signal type/model class).
- C9 (champion-challenger promotion gate): §2.4 names it explicitly as "widely documented MLOps practice," citing DataRobot/Snowflake.
- C6 (export-format loss mechanism): §2.1 states the community-documented GitHub-issue prior art explicitly and frames the paper's contribution as "quantifying that suspected mechanism... not discovering it."
- C10 (sturgeon-specificity): §2.1 hedges correctly — "the sturgeon-specific prior work is thin enough... that a working, measured, sturgeon-specific decision pipeline is itself a modest but genuine contribution," which matches the recommended cautious framing ("no evidence of a directly prior instance was found") rather than an unqualified first-system claim.
- The one `OPEN` claim with medium confidence (C2, cross-layer consistency) is exactly the claim §2.5 foregrounds as "the honest novelty locus," consistent with the lit-review's own recommendation to elevate it as the headline claim.

I found no sentence in the final text where novelty framing has regressed relative to what the adversarial review established — the "nice-to-fix" reviewer suggestion (state the known-prior-art point in the Abstract itself, not only in Related Work) was not taken, and the Abstract still lets a reader reach its final sentence without an explicit "this pattern is not new" clause. That is a real, if minor, residual gap: a reader who stops at the Abstract could momentarily overestimate architectural novelty before reaching §2.2. It was correctly logged as nice-to-fix rather than a blocker in the review, and I agree with that severity — the paper's title, framing verbs ("this paper reports what happened when that architecture was measured, not designed"), and the Introduction's own hedged language ("this paper does not claim to have solved LLM unreliability") already lean away from a novelty claim before Related Work is ever reached, so the risk of a lasting false impression is low, not zero.

## Logical Gaps

Nothing rises to the level of a logical gap that undermines a headline claim. Two sub-blocker observations, both already present in the review's own nice-to-fix list and neither actioned in the current text, worth naming for round 2 of this persona review:

1. The Abstract's closing sentence — "such a pipeline can be made safe against an unreliable model provided its validator reads nothing beyond one enumerable field and always defaults conservatively" — is true as stated but sits one clause away from a reader conflating it with "safe against fabricated reasoning," which Formalization 2 explicitly disclaims protection against. The paper polices this distinction rigorously everywhere else (it is the entire point of Formalization 2 and the EF1 fabrication finding), so this is not a factual error, just a missed opportunity for the Abstract's final sentence to pre-empt the most obvious misreading of itself, exactly as Reviewer Question 2 already flagged.
2. The live, unfixed 0.695 RAG knowledge-base figure remains unfixed in the text as of this reading (Known Limitations still describes it as "not fixed during this audit"). This is disclosed honestly and repeatedly, not a rigor problem with the paper's claims — but it is worth flagging that "ready for submission" and "the underlying system still serves stale safety-adjacent information to its own retrieval layer" are two different questions, and this paper answers only the first one for itself while leaving the second as a live, named TODO against the deployed system it describes.

Neither of these is a hard blocker, and neither touches the $n=8$ scoping or adapter-scoping checks this round was specifically tasked with verifying — both of which pass cleanly.
