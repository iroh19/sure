## Assessment

The proposal's argument-level work is stronger than Round 1's and does not repeat praise I already gave: the monotonicity formalization is correct as far as it goes, and I verified independently that `backend/main.py:apply_rule_override` (not `inference.py`, which never imports `rules`) is where the real escalate-only merge happens, gated on `SEVERITY.get(parsed.get("status"), 0)` with missing/unknown status defaulting to 0 — exactly the "malformed output resolves to the safe side" case H1's three-case argument requires, and it is unit-tested directly (`backend/test_decision.py::test_override_escalates_when_model_misses_critical`, `::test_override_treats_missing_status_as_ok`, `::test_override_treats_unknown_status_as_ok`). That is real evidence for the *mechanism's logical correctness* — a stronger claim than Round 1 had available, because Round 1 hadn't been shown these tests exist.

But mechanism correctness under synthetic fixtures is a different claim from H1's actual predicted observable ("when AQUA-1B is run through eval.py in model mode, every scenario in which the model's severity is a strict under-call ... will show a corrected, escalated final severity"), and here I found a concrete instrumentation gap the proposal does not notice: `eval.py`'s `model_status()` calls `inference.generate_decision(snapshot)` directly and compares it to `rule_status()` — it never calls `apply_rule_override`. Running Goal 1 exactly as specified will produce a raw LLM/rule-engine *disagreement rate*, not the escalated *final severity* H1 predicts. Those are related but distinct quantities, and the gap between them is precisely the "is the LLM narration-only or does an override event actually change the displayed status" question the whole paper hinges on. As written, Goal 1 will not by itself answer H1's stated prediction; a second small step (running the scenario set back through `apply_rule_override`, or adding this to `eval.py`) is needed and is currently untracked.

The proposal's small-sample handling has also not moved past "report exact counts" even where a cheap fix for the underlying thinness was sitting in the code. `bench_agent.py` has a `--repeat N` flag built for exactly this purpose (the module docstring even lists `python -m agent.bench_agent --repeat 3` as a usage example), and using it would turn the AQUA-1B format-compliance measurement from n=5 into n=15+ at zero infrastructure cost — no new dependency, no new physical experiment, just more inference calls on a model already loaded. Goal 7 commits only to reporting the existing 5-scenario counts with more precision language, not to widening them with a flag the tool authors evidently built for this. This is the kind of "acknowledged in prose, not resolved with the cheapest available fix" pattern the task asked me to hunt for, and I found a concrete instance of it.

## Novelty Analysis

- No change to my Round 1 position on the closest-work comparison (YOLO-family aquaculture CV, CoreML/ANE export benchmarking, LLM-advisor-with-deterministic-override guardrail patterns, PSI-based drift gating) — I will not re-litigate what I already accepted as sufficiently distinct.
- **New this round:** the proposal's H3 (deterministic tool-routing over LLM agentic routing, evidence-driven not precautionary) is, on inspection of `bench_agent.py`, a *stronger* novelty claim than the proposal states, because the benchmark's "constant-answer detection" logic (flagging AQUA-7B's 50%/60% selection numbers as an artifact of always picking `get_sensor_trend` rather than genuine discrimination) is itself a small methodological contribution worth naming explicitly as a unit of novelty — a naive published benchmark would have reported "AQUA-7B: 50% tool selection accuracy" and stopped, treating an appealing-looking aggregate as competence. The proposal mentions this fact (Core Hypothesis H3) but files it under "predicted observable consequence" rather than claiming the *detection technique itself* (checking `len(set(chosen)) == 1` across ≥3 outputs before trusting a selection percentage) as a transferable evaluation-methodology contribution. This is free novelty the proposal is leaving on the table, not a request for new work.
- The "twin_bridge as independent-implementation cross-validation" claim (Sub-question 2) remains the proposal's most interesting *unrealized* novelty claim — two independently-authored rule systems (Python `rules.py` vs. IEC 61131-3 Structured Text on CODESYS) agreeing on a live plant is a genuinely stronger corroboration argument than anything else in the paper, which is exactly why it deserves more scrutiny about whether it will actually be exercised (see below), not less.

## Logical Gaps

- **New: Goal 1's instrumentation does not test H1's stated prediction.** As detailed in Assessment — `eval.py` in model mode measures LLM/rule disagreement, not `apply_rule_override`'s escalated final severity. The proposal conflates these. Fix is cheap (a few lines) but is currently absent from the tracked plan.
- **New: the `apply_rule_override` unit tests use hand-constructed `parsed` dicts, not real model output.** They prove the merge function is logically sound given *any* status string; they say nothing about what status strings AQUA-1B actually emits in practice, how often it's unparseable, or whether the "narration-only" alternative explanation (Round 1's Alt-Explanation #3) is true. The proposal should not let the existence of these unit tests be read (by itself or a future reader) as partial evidence toward H1's empirical claim — they are mechanism tests, not behavior measurements, and the two are easy to conflate in a systems paper under deadline pressure.
- **New: the `twin_bridge` fallback plan names an artifact that does not exist.** Risk row 2 says "if no replayable session exists, generate one from an existing recorded demo session." I searched the repo for any capture file matching the `--replay` JSON schema (`{"holding": [...], "input": [...]}`) and found none. Producing one requires standing up `ras-digital-twin-main` (gitignored, requires the Godot 4 engine binary — not installed in this environment — and a running CODESYS soft PLC per `plc/SETUP.md`, a proprietary IDE product with a manual GUI setup step) as a live two-process Modbus session, then writing a capture script that does not currently exist anywhere in `twin_bridge/` or `ras-digital-twin-main/` (the twin's own self-test dumps a CSV trend via `--exportcsv`, not the raw register-frame JSON `compare.py --replay` consumes). This is not "generate one from an existing recorded demo session" — it is standing up new real-time infrastructure and writing a new adapter script, which sits closer to "new physical experiment" than to "additional analysis of already-existing artifacts," a distinction the project's own framing explicitly tries to preserve. The proposal should either scope this honestly as a stretch goal with an explicit go/no-go date, or drop the confident "run `twin_bridge/compare.py --replay`" verb from Goal 2 in favor of "stand up the twin and capture a session, if time permits; otherwise report as designed-but-unexercised in Limitations" — which is closer to what Risk row 2 already half-says, but the Empirical Plan's Goal 2 doesn't carry the same hedge.
- **Unresolved from Round 1, now compounded:** the adapter-provenance ambiguity (possibly still the 8-sample v1 LoRA, not the untested 128-sample v2) is repeated verbatim in the "Honest scoping" paragraph, but Goal 1's model-mode eval — if finally run — will silently inherit whichever adapter happens to be in `sure-aqua-adapter/` right now (confirmed present, dated ~June 5) without the proposal committing to *check which version that is* before reporting numbers. A single `git log`/file-timestamp check against `finetune.py`'s default-output-path history would resolve this in minutes and should be a zero-cost sub-step of Goal 1, not a separate unresolved caveat sitting three sections away.
- **Not new but still unaddressed:** Round 1's Ablation #8 (AQUA-1B vs. AQUA-7B decision-*quality*, not just format compliance) is retained as Goal 8, but nothing in the revised proposal specifies what "decision quality" is scored against beyond the same 8 rule-engine-labeled scenarios already used for H1 — if the metric is still "did the model's status match the rule engine's status," Goal 8 collapses into a re-run of Goal 1 with a bigger model rather than a genuinely separate quality measure (e.g., of the `reasoning` field's citation accuracy or clinical plausibility). This ambiguity was present in Round 1 too and has not been resolved by giving it a number.

## Alternative Explanations

1. **The escalation mechanism is logically sound (proven by unit tests) but empirically may almost never fire**, because AQUA-1B may mostly fail to produce parseable output at all (consistent with the 0% agent-bench finding) — in which case the dominant real-world pathway is "malformed output defaults to ok, rule engine independently computes the real status," which is a materially weaker and different claim than "the LLM reasons, sometimes under-calls, and gets corrected." *Discriminating analysis:* Required Ablation #1, run properly (see Logical Gaps), directly separates these two pathways by classifying each of the 8 scenarios into {parseable-and-agrees, parseable-and-under-calls-and-escalated, unparseable-defaulted-to-ok}.
2. **`apply_rule_override`'s unit-test suite could be mistaken (by a future co-author under deadline pressure) for empirical evidence of H1**, since both use the word "escalate" and live near each other in the codebase narrative. *Discriminating analysis/caveat:* the manuscript must explicitly label the unit tests as "mechanism verification under synthetic model-output fixtures" and keep this separate from any claim about observed model behavior.
3. **The `twin_bridge` module's clean design (explicit `EXPECTED_DIVERGENCE` vocabulary, `agree`/`expected`/`unexplained` taxonomy) could be mistaken for evidence that cross-validation has already happened**, since the code reads as production-ready. *Discriminating analysis:* it has never been run against a live or replayed session (git status confirms `twin_bridge/` is untracked with only `.pyc` cache files, no result artifacts); the manuscript must not let well-written, well-commented, *unexecuted* code stand in for a result.
4. **Goal 7's "report exact counts" for the agent benchmark could read as if sample-size concerns were resolved**, when in fact n is unchanged from Round 1 (5 scenarios × 1 repeat by default) unless Required Ablation #3 is actually run. *Discriminating analysis/caveat:* do not describe the small-sample mitigation as "addressed" in the Limitations section unless `--repeat` was actually used; if it wasn't, say so plainly rather than relying on "exact counts reported" to imply the concern was handled.

## Verdict

The proposal's argument structure is genuinely stronger than Round 1's (a real monotonicity formalization, a bounded H1/H2 pair, an evidence-driven H3 framing), and I verified independently that the core escalation mechanism is unit-tested and logically sound — that is real progress, not just prose. But on inspection, several of the "concrete, trackable research goals" are less concrete than they read: Goal 1 (the paper's most important open item) has had zero blocking dependencies since before Round 1 yet remains unexecuted, and as specified would not even measure what H1 predicts; Goal 2's fallback plan invents an artifact that does not exist and understates the infrastructure lift required; and Goal 7's sample-size mitigation ignores a zero-cost fix (`--repeat`) the codebase already provides. None of this is disqualifying — the underlying architecture and integration-discipline novelty claim remain sound and well-motivated, and every gap I found has a cheap, nameable fix — but it means the proposal has not yet earned the higher bar this round asked for. I am accepting conditionally again, but I am flagging that "ACCEPT with tracked gaps" cannot be the answer a third time running if Goal 1 in particular is still unexecuted at Round 3 despite having no external blocker.

VERDICT: ACCEPT
