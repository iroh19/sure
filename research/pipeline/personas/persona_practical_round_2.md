## Assessment

This proposal did real work, not cosmetic work, on most of my Round 1 asks. The eval-harness drift bug now opens the Narrative Arc exactly where I asked for it — before the architecture diagram, as the motivating incident. The agent tool-use negative result (AQUA-1B: 0%/0%; AQUA-7B's 50% exposed as a constant-answer artifact) has been promoted from an implementation footnote to a first-class hypothesis (H3) with its own Results-subsection commitment. The PSI equal-width-vs-quantile binning failure and the RAG asymmetric-cost threshold are both now stated as general, transferable principles with explicit "Conditions:" fields — precisely the "under what architectures, at what scale, with what trade-offs" specificity this persona's mandate demands. The Risk Assessment table is honest about the two biggest open empirical bets (model-mode `eval.py`, `twin_bridge` replay) rather than assuming they'll land favorably. This is a genuine second draft, not a restatement of the task list with hedges added.

But checking harder against what I actually asked for in Round 1 turns up one significant new problem and two Round 1 asks that were re-labeled rather than satisfied. The significant new problem: in synthesizing "one design philosophy, four tests" as the paper's spine, the proposal has quietly demoted the entire **Edge Vision Pipeline** — vision.md's contribution #2, and the source of two of my own Round 1 top-3 practitioner findings (the systematic fp32-export accuracy loss, and the CoreML/ANE latency win) — out of the Central Research Question, out of every Core Hypothesis (H1–H5 cover only decision/agent/RAG/MLOps), and out of the Narrative Arc's four-beat structure. It survives only as a line item in the "existing metrics to report" list and an Ablation/Control row. The Vision Coverage Map still marks it "COVERED," but coverage-as-a-reported-table is not the same as coverage-as-a-tested-claim, and a senior engineer skimming this paper for its thesis will not encounter the export-benchmarking finding as a thesis-level result — only as a data table. That is exactly the kind of unjustified narrowing this round's mandate asks me to catch: a vision element didn't get argued out of scope, it fell out because it doesn't cleanly fit the unifying "probabilistic-component/deterministic-authority" frame the synthesis converged on, and no one seems to have noticed the fit problem rather than just accepting the drop.

The two re-labelings: my Round 1 ask for explicit training/inference hardware detail (M4 Pro, training time for YOLOv11s and the LoRA adapter) is simply absent from this proposal — not rejected, not deferred with reasoning, just not carried forward. And my ask for a reader-facing "Known Limitations" section was answered with an internal "Risk Assessment" table and an "Honest scoping" paragraph — useful planning artifacts, but a risk table with likelihood/impact/mitigation columns is pAI's own project-management tool, not a commitment that the manuscript itself will contain a limitations section a reviewer can point to. These aren't fatal, but they are exactly the "papered over" pattern I was told to check for this round, and I'm flagging both explicitly so they don't quietly disappear again in Round 3.

## Scope vs. Vision

1. **Does the proposal address the full scope of the vision?** Mostly, and better than Round 1's raw task capture in three respects: it recovers the agent tool-use negative result and the `twin_bridge` PLC cross-validation module into first-class research questions (both were previously buried or absent from vision.md itself), and it adds explicit generalization-boundary hypotheses (H2) that vision.md never asked for but that make the central claim falsifiable rather than asserted. That is genuine scope *expansion* beyond the raw vision, which is what Round 2 should look like.

2. **Justified deepening or unjustified retreat?** Both, in different places. The consolidation of the LLM-severity, agent-routing, RAG, and MLOps layers into one testable design philosophy (H1–H5) is a justified deepening — it is richer, more falsifiable treatment of exactly the elements vision.md names, not fewer of them. But the resulting demotion of the Edge Vision Pipeline out of the RQ/Hypothesis/Narrative spine is an unjustified retreat, even though it wasn't done consciously to avoid difficulty — it's a side effect of forcing every vision element through a single unifying lens that this one element does not naturally fit. The fix is not to abandon the unifying thesis; it's to either (a) find the genuine version of the thesis that the vision layer instantiates, or (b) explicitly carry it as a second, clearly-labeled strand of equal main-body weight rather than letting the four-layer framing implicitly relegate it to a metrics appendix.

3. **Vision elements not yet covered (research goals, not cuts):**
   - **The Edge Vision Pipeline as a tested claim, not just a reported table.** There is a real hypothesis hiding here that the proposal hasn't stated: detector confidence and count outputs feed directly into `rules.py`'s deterministic thresholds (e.g., a `fish_count == 0` rule), so the vision layer's precision/recall operating-point choice (0.858/0.719) is itself an instance of the same deterministic-consumes-probabilistic-output pattern — recall errors in dense frames are a probabilistic failure mode that the downstream rule engine's threshold logic must be shown to tolerate or fail to tolerate. As written, the proposal treats vision accuracy and the export-benchmarking finding as freestanding metrics with no connection to the paper's central claim. That connection should become an explicit H6, not be left implicit.
   - **Training/inference hardware and compute budget**, requested in Round 1, still not committed to any deliverable in this proposal (see Critical Gaps).
   - **A manuscript-facing Known Limitations section**, requested in Round 1 as a required *reader-visible* element, currently exists only as internal planning artifacts (Risk Assessment table, "Honest scoping" prose scattered through Methodology) with no commitment that these will consolidate into a section a reviewer can cite.

## Structural Recommendation

Re-ranking by practitioner-workflow impact, checked against what the proposal currently promotes to main-body status:

1. **Dual-layer rule-override + the eval-harness drift bug.** Correctly placed as the opening of the Narrative Arc. No change needed — this is the one finding that must survive to the final draft's Introduction untouched.
2. **The agent tool-use negative result (H3).** Correctly promoted to hypothesis-level, correctly framed as folklore-busting evidence rather than an implementation detail. This is now the proposal's strongest structural improvement over Round 1.
3. **The systematic fp32-export accuracy loss (four-decimal-place match between ONNX and TorchScript, implicating the post-processing path, not precision).** This is currently **not** promoted to hypothesis level and is at real risk of ending up as a table row rather than the "measure the whole pipeline, not just the weights" principle it actually is. It needs its own subsection commitment now, in the proposal, or it will not survive to the draft.
4. **PSI quantile-vs-equal-width binning (H5).** Correctly promoted. No change needed.
5. **RAG asymmetric-cost threshold (H4).** Correctly promoted. No change needed.
6. **CoreML/ANE latency table.** Fine as a secondary, single-platform-scoped result; correctly caveated in the Ablation table (Row 1, thermal/variance control). No promotion needed beyond what exists.

If a senior ML engineer reads only one section, the export-accuracy finding (item 3) is currently the one most likely to get cut in a length-constrained draft, precisely because it has no hypothesis number and no Narrative Arc sentence — and it is one of the two or three findings in this entire system that most directly contradicts a widespread practitioner assumption (export is a no-op; quantization is where accuracy is paid). That must not be allowed to happen by default.

## Critical Gaps

- **Edge Vision Pipeline (vision.md contribution #2) is absent from the Central Research Question, all five Core Hypotheses, and the Narrative Arc.** It exists only as a metrics-table line item and two Ablation rows. This is a structural narrowing of the paper's spine that the Vision Coverage Map's "COVERED" label obscures rather than surfaces.
- **Training/inference hardware and compute-time detail** (requested Round 1, Specific Suggestion #4) is not present anywhere in this proposal — not addressed, not explicitly deferred with a reason. Practitioners judging reproducibility/adoptability need this and it costs nothing to include (the number, M4 Pro / ~1-2 hours, already exists in the repo's own MODEL_RAPORU.md).
- **"Known Limitations" as a reader-facing section** (requested Round 1) has been satisfied only by internal planning artifacts (Risk Assessment table, scattered "Honest scoping" prose). Nothing in this proposal commits to a Discussion/Conclusion subsection a reviewer can point to and confirm exists.
- **No dollar/resource framing for the local-LLM choice.** The paper's central thesis is partly "keep a small local LLM but don't trust it with the final call" — a practitioner deciding whether to replicate this will want to know what running AQUA-1B/7B locally costs in memory/compute versus a cloud API call, even briefly. This is not in vision.md and may be legitimate scope discipline to exclude, but it should be an explicit decision, not a silent omission.
- **No consideration of what a `twin_bridge`/rule-engine *disagreement* would mean for the safety claim.** Sub-question 2 and Risk Row 2 both treat the twin-bridge check as a corroboration exercise ("does it agree") but neither states what conclusion the paper draws if the two implementations meaningfully diverge on a real scenario — this needs a stated interpretation plan before the check is run, not after, so the result isn't rationalized post hoc.

## Specific Suggestions

1. Add an explicit H6 (or fold into H1's mechanism): "Deterministic thresholds in `rules.py` consume probabilistic vision-layer output (fish count, detection confidence) as one of their probabilistic inputs, and the vision layer's precision/recall operating point (0.858/0.719 at the F1-maximizing confidence) is chosen to bound the failure mode the downstream rule logic must tolerate (e.g., a false-zero fish count during a dense-frame occlusion)." This gives the Edge Vision Pipeline a genuine seat at the thesis table instead of leaving it as a freestanding metrics report.
2. Give the systematic fp32-export accuracy loss (four-decimal match between ONNX/TorchScript export) an explicit hypothesis number and a named Results subsection commitment, parallel to H1–H5 — currently it is the single Round-1-flagged top finding most at risk of being cut for length.
3. Add one line to the Empirical Plan's "existing metrics to report" list naming the training hardware/time for YOLOv11s and the LoRA adapter (already documented in MODEL_RAPORU.md) — zero new work, closes a Round 1 gap that was silently dropped rather than addressed.
4. Convert "Honest scoping to be stated explicitly throughout" and the Risk Assessment table's caveats into an explicit commitment: "the manuscript will include a dedicated Known Limitations subsection in the Discussion/Conclusion, drawing directly from this table" — make the deliverable, not just the planning artifact, part of the proposal.
5. Before running `twin_bridge/compare.py --replay`, state in the proposal what the paper will conclude in each of the three possible outcomes (full agreement, partial/explicable divergence, unexplained divergence) — this prevents the interpretation being written only after seeing a favorable result.
6. Consider whether the local-LLM compute/cost framing belongs in scope; if excluded, state that decision explicitly rather than leaving it unaddressed.
