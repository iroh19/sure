## One-Paragraph Pitch

*(New this round, added at Narrative's request as an elevator-pitch sketch to seed the eventual Abstract and to keep the proposal's growing apparatus below from overwhelming the story it supports — practitioner-facing, no jargon.)*

A team monitoring sturgeon in an indoor fish farm built a small on-site AI to watch water quality and fish behavior around the clock, because a bad water reading can kill an entire tank of fish within hours. They did not let that AI have the final word: a simple, fully-tested rule checker reviews everything the AI reports and is allowed to raise the alarm level if the AI misses something serious — it can never lower one. The team applied that same principle — let the simple, checkable system have the last word — everywhere else they used AI too: in how the system decides which sensor or camera reading to check next, in how it searches its reference documents, in when it retrains its camera model, and in how much it trusts that camera model's own numbers in the first place. In one case they tested giving the AI more independence, it simply failed the test, and they kept the simpler, checkable version instead of assuming more autonomy would be better. The paper's core claim is that when AI reasoning meets a decision that can cost lives or stock, a small, inspectable, rule-based backstop — not a bigger or more autonomous model — is what actually keeps the system safe, and this paper measures exactly where that backstop holds, where it does not yet, and what it costs.

## Research Question

**Central Research Question:** In a deployed edge-AI aquaculture welfare monitoring system, does giving deterministic, code-shared logic sole, escalation-only final authority over five architecturally distinct, independently probabilistic surfaces — LLM severity reasoning, LLM-driven agentic tool routing, RAG retrieval, MLOps retrain triggering, and the edge vision detection pipeline that supplies all four of these with their raw sensor input — measurably reduce safety-relevant failure modes relative to trusting each surface's own probabilistic output, and at what documented cost to coverage, recall, or measurement rigor in each layer?

H6 is a sibling *evidence-discipline* claim — verifying accuracy end-to-end across every export/deployment target rather than assuming it survives optimization for speed — that does not itself fit the override frame and must not be force-fit into it for the sake of a clean "five-for-five" symmetry. **Sub-questions:**
1. What is the *observed* (not merely architecturally guaranteed) rate at which the deterministic rule engine overrides or escalates AQUA-1B's raw severity judgment when the model is actually exercised, and does the LLM contribute decision-relevant reasoning beyond narration?
2. Does the independently-implemented `twin_bridge` PLC/CODESYS validation path corroborate `backend/rules.py`'s decisions, providing cross-implementation evidence for the safety claim beyond single-implementation self-consistency — and, separately from any live session, does `backend/test_decision.py`'s unit-test suite already establish the escalation mechanism's *logical* correctness under synthetic fixtures?
3. Does the same "deterministic-authority-over-probabilistic-component" design principle generalize as a coherent pattern across the RAG and MLOps layers, and what are its boundary conditions — specifically, does it hold for enumerable, threshold-based failure modes but not for non-enumerable, open-ended judgment failures?
4. Does the vision layer's chosen operating point (precision 0.858 / recall 0.719) and its export/quantization pipeline (CoreML/ANE vs. PyTorch/ONNX CPU, INT8 quantization) hold up under the same "measure the whole pipeline, don't assume" scrutiny applied to the other layers — and what does its residual ~28% per-instance miss rate imply for the one deterministic rule (`fish_count == 0`) that directly consumes vision output, given that rule operates on total-frame absence rather than per-instance recall?

## Motivation & Field Context

Precision aquaculture and RAS (recirculating aquaculture systems) monitoring is moving from manual observation toward automated, sensor-fused decision support, driven by the same economic pressure (labor scarcity, disease/DO-crash losses measured in hours) that has pushed precision livestock and precision agriculture generally toward edge AI. Three trends make this exact question timely rather than incidental:

1. **Edge AI deployment reality.** Locally-hosted vision (YOLO-family detectors) and now locally-hosted small LLMs (Gemma-class, Mistral-class, sub-2B parameter, on-device via MLX/CoreML/ANE or quantized CPU/GPU) are becoming the default for connectivity-constrained agricultural and industrial sites. CoreML/ANE and Jetson-class benchmarking of these pipelines is common, but export-format accuracy-versus-latency studies rarely hold the *entire* validation methodology constant across every export target the way this system's `export_bench.py` does — most public benchmarks report speed without re-measuring accuracy per format, and fewer still notice when two independently-exported formats (ONNX, TorchScript) match each other's accuracy loss to four decimal places, which is itself evidence about *where* the loss originates (a shared post-processing/NMS code path) rather than a property of either export's numeric precision.
2. **LLM safety/hallucination-mitigation discourse.** 2025–2026 applied-AI discourse on "LLM agents in safety-critical settings" is dominated by two extremes: hype ("agents will run the plant autonomously") and vague hand-wringing ("we need guardrails"). Guardrail literature that does exist (constitutional AI, output validators, LLM-as-advisor patterns in fintech/medical triage) typically re-validates model output via a *second model* or a *schema check*, not via a domain-specific, dependency-free deterministic rule engine with formal escalation-only precedence enforced by literal code-sharing between the production path and the evaluation harness. S.U.R.E. is a rare case of this pattern already built, deployed, and measured — including a *documented* motivating failure (an eval harness whose copied rule logic silently drifted from production and reported green while validating nothing), which is a more persuasive artifact than any abstract argument about "LLM reliability."
3. **The "is the pipeline actually measured, or just the model" gap.** A YOLO detector's headline mAP is routinely reported as a fixed model property; it is in fact a chosen operating point (precision/recall tradeoff at a confidence threshold) whose downstream safety consequences are rarely traced through to the consuming logic. This system's rule engine is a concrete, inspectable consumer of that operating point (`fish_count == 0` → escalate), which makes it possible — and, per this round's revision, mandatory — to state what the vision layer's recall actually implies for the safety architecture rather than reporting it as an isolated benchmark number.

The paper's contribution is therefore not any individual component (YOLOv11s, ByteTrack, e5-small, pgvector, PSI are all pre-existing tools) but the **integration discipline**: a consistent, evidence-driven decision — at four architecturally distinct layers — to keep the probabilistic component in an advisory role and give a deterministic, testable component final authority, with the tradeoffs of that decision measured and disclosed rather than assumed; and a sibling discipline, applied to the vision pipeline specifically, of verifying accuracy end-to-end across every deployment target rather than trusting that an accuracy number measured once on trained weights survives export and quantization. Because this discipline is demonstrated with one full negative result (an on-device LLM shown, by benchmark, to be incapable of reliable agentic tool-calling) rather than argued from precaution, it directly engages — and can push back against — the "more LLM autonomy is more capability" folklore that is currently under-evidenced in both directions in the applied-AI literature. It also pushes back against a second, quieter folklore item made explicit this round: "quantization is where you pay for edge speed" — this system's own numbers show the fp32 CPU export path already pays a larger accuracy cost than INT8 quantization adds on top of it.

**Folklore-naming note (New this round — explicit deferral, not a silent drop).** Narrative's Round 2 review named four additional folklore/field-assumption statements this proposal engages with only implicitly rather than as named rebuttal sentences: "automation trades off against safety"; "higher retrieval benchmark scores mean better production RAG" (the "arithmetic, not skill" framing); "drift detected implies retrain warranted" ("drift alarm ≠ retrain mandate"); and "an evaluation harness that agrees with itself is validating the system." Rather than fold these into named rebuttal prose now on persona-council authority alone, they are explicitly deferred to the literature_review phase immediately following this council's exit — that phase is better positioned to surface the actual field/citation evidence behind each folklore claim than a three-persona debate can. This is a stated deferral, not a dropped request.

## Core Hypotheses

1. **H1 — Escalation-only override reduces missed-escalation risk for enumerable thresholds.** *Mechanism:* `backend/rules.py` is imported unmodified by both the production decision path and the evaluation harness, and severity is only ever escalated, never lowered, relative to the LLM's stated severity; the actual escalate-only merge is performed by `backend/main.py`'s `apply_rule_override`, which computes `final_severity` from `SEVERITY.get(parsed.get("status"), 0)` so that missing or unparseable model output defaults to the safe (`ok`, i.e. lowest-precedence) side rather than blocking. *Conditions:* holds when the failure mode is a hard, enumerable, numeric/categorical threshold (e.g., dissolved oxygen < 6 mg/L). *Predicted observable consequence:* when the 8 `eval.py` scenarios' raw AQUA-1B output is passed through `apply_rule_override` (see Methodology, Goal 1 — rewritten this round to actually test this), every scenario in which the model's severity is a strict under-call relative to the sensor-computed rule severity will show a corrected, escalated final severity, and no scenario will show the final severity below what the rule engine alone would produce. `backend/test_decision.py`'s existing unit tests (`test_override_escalates_when_model_misses_critical`, `test_override_treats_missing_status_as_ok`, `test_override_treats_unknown_status_as_ok`) already establish this mechanism is *logically* sound under synthetic fixtures — that is real evidence for correctness-of-design, but it is not yet evidence about what AQUA-1B actually emits in practice, which is a distinct, currently unmeasured claim (see Goal 1).
2. **H2 — The mechanism does not extend to non-enumerable failure modes.** *Mechanism:* the rule engine has no representation of free-text reasoning quality, chat correctness, or judgments outside its coded threshold set. *Conditions:* holds whenever the failure is in the LLM's prose explanation, its citation accuracy, or a domain judgment not reducible to a coded rule. *Predicted observable consequence:* the paper's central claim must be scoped to "escalation-only deterministic override for enumerable safety thresholds," not general hallucination mitigation — a claim broader than this is falsified by the RAG layer's own residual hallucination risk at any similarity threshold below 1.0.
3. **H3 — Deterministic tool-routing outperforms LLM-driven agentic tool selection at the deployed model scale.** *Mechanism:* small (~1B parameter) locally-hosted models under a step-budgeted agent loop fail to reliably emit schema-valid, selectively-appropriate tool calls. *Conditions:* holds for AQUA-1B (Gemma 3 1B) under the current prompt/scenario set; not yet established for larger on-device models. *Predicted observable consequence:* `bench_agent.py`'s existing measurement (AQUA-1B: 0% parseable-action format, 0% correct selection, identical output across four scenarios in a prefill test) is the direct evidence; AQUA-7B's headline 50% selection rate is revealed as a "constant answer" artifact (`get_sensor_trend` chosen in all five scenarios) rather than genuine competence. The benchmark's own constant-answer detection technique (flagging a selection percentage as an artifact when `len(set(chosen)) == 1` across ≥3 outputs, rather than trusting the aggregate) is itself a small, transferable evaluation-methodology contribution, separate from the negative result it uncovered, and should be named as such rather than left implicit in a footnote.
4. **H4 — A precision-biased RAG threshold reduces hallucinated-citation risk at a quantified recall cost.** *Mechanism:* similarity threshold selection driven by an explicit positive/hard-negative sweep and an asymmetric cost function (a missed document only weakens reasoning the rule engine still adjudicates; a fabricated citation actively misinforms). *Conditions:* holds specifically for this 8-document/44-chunk Turkish-language aquaculture corpus. *Predicted observable consequence:* at threshold 0.85, hard-negative pass-through drops to 0/12 (from 3/12 at 0.84) at the cost of positive recall dropping from 29/29 to 24/29 (F1 0.906 vs. the F1-optimal 0.951 at 0.84).
5. **H5 — Quantile-edge PSI binning detects graded drift where equal-width binning produces a degenerate binary signal.** *Mechanism:* the detector's confidence outputs are concentrated in a narrow empirical range (~0.5–0.9); equal-width bins over [0,1] leave several bins empty in the reference distribution, so any mass shift into an empty bin saturates PSI immediately. *Conditions:* holds for any confidence-style output metric concentrated in a narrow real-valued band. *Predicted observable consequence:* the existing measurement shows equal-width binning jumping from PSI 0.06 to 1.05 with no intermediate signal, while quantile-edge binning grades smoothly (0.039 → 0.098 → 0.265 → 8.28) across increasing synthetic degradation.
6. **H6 — NEW (restores Edge Vision to hypothesis level). Export-format accuracy loss is systematic and post-processing-attributable, not a stochastic "quantization tax."** *Mechanism:* `export_bench.py` holds the validation methodology constant across every export target; ONNX/CPU and TorchScript/CPU exports match each other's mAP50 (0.8291) and precision loss to four decimal places relative to the pt/mps baseline (0.8395), which implicates a shared post-processing/NMS code path common to both exports rather than a numeric-precision effect specific to either format. INT8 quantization on top of ONNX shows a mAP50 of 0.8313 (Δ −0.0082 vs. baseline) — a *smaller* accuracy loss than the fp32 ONNX/TorchScript exports already pay (Δ −0.0104) before quantization is even applied. *Conditions:* holds for this YOLOv11s/Ultralytics export toolchain measured against this 98-image validation set; the finding is a toolchain-specific diagnosis, not a general claim about ONNX/TorchScript export correctness, and should not be generalized to other detector architectures without re-verification. *Predicted observable consequence:* reporting the full six-configuration table (pt/mps, pt/cpu, onnx/cpu, onnx-int8/cpu, coreml/ANE, torchscript/cpu — latency, FPS, ΔmAP50, model size) will show CoreML/ANE achieving p50 9.0 ms (111 FPS) / p95 9.5 ms, ~3.5× faster than PyTorch/MPS and roughly 3× smaller, while paying no larger an accuracy cost than the CPU export paths already do independent of quantization — directly falsifying "quantization is where you pay for edge speed" as applied to this pipeline, and substantiating "measure the whole exported pipeline, not just the trained weights" as the actionable practitioner guideline.
7. **H7 — NEW (connects Edge Vision to the paper's central thesis). The vision layer's operating point is itself a probabilistic input that a deterministic rule must tolerate, and the tolerance boundary is narrower than the headline recall number implies.** *Mechanism:* `backend/rules.py`'s only vision-derived rule triggers on total frame-level absence (`fish_count == 0`, which escalates to `warning` — deliberately not `critical`, because the rule text itself encodes the ambiguity between "vision service failed" and "shoal is at the bottom of the tank" and cannot distinguish them). This rule operates on complete per-frame absence, not on the per-instance detection miss rate that the reported recall of 0.719 actually measures. *Conditions:* holds for this single-class, 510-image, single-tank dataset and this confidence operating point (precision 0.858 / recall 0.719, chosen to maximize F1). *Predicted observable consequence — the "so what" this round adds:* because the one existing vision-consuming rule is a total-absence check rather than a per-instance-count threshold, a systematic ~28% per-instance undercount does **not**, on inspection of the current rule set, trip any explicit safety escalation unless it happens to zero an entire frame — meaning the practical safety exposure of the current recall figure is that partial undercounting can silently degrade the `avg_activity`-derived low-activity rule (which depends on a tracked count) without ever surfacing as its own disclosed risk. This is reported here as an honest, disclosed gap (the vision layer's recall has a safety-relevant blind spot that no current rule directly gates), not resolved by claiming a downstream safeguard that does not exist in the code as inspected.

## Methodology Overview

### Analysis/Theory-equivalent Plan

The paper will formalize, as precise definitions and architectural arguments (not new proofs, but rigor-grade formalization of what is already implemented):

- **Deterministic authority**, defined as a precedence relation over a finite severity lattice, where the final decision is `final_severity = max(severity_rule, severity_llm)` under a total order on severity levels — formalizing the "escalate-only, never downgrade" invariant as a monotonicity property, and arguing its correctness depends on `rules.py` having zero shared mutable state or dependency divergence from the production import path (the property that the eval-harness drift bug violated and that the current single-source-of-truth refactor restores).
- **The escalation-override mechanism** as a fail-safe composition: malformed LLM output, LLM service unavailability, and a correctly-parsed but under-severe LLM judgment are three distinct failure inputs that must all resolve to the same or a more conservative final decision — the paper will state this as an explicit three-case argument rather than leaving it implicit in the architecture diagram, and will explicitly separate **mechanism verification** (the `test_decision.py` unit-test suite, which proves the merge function is logically sound given *any* status string, under synthetic fixtures) from **behavioral measurement** (what AQUA-1B actually emits in practice) — the manuscript must never let the former stand in as evidence for the latter.
- **"Enumerable" vs. "non-enumerable" failure modes**, defined precisely (a finite, coded, checkable predicate over sensor/state variables vs. an open-ended natural-language judgment), to bound the scope of the central claim per H2 and the Narrative reviewer's Precision Check.
- **The PSI-gating rule**, formalized as: reference distribution → quantile-edge binning (deciles of the reference) → PSI computation over a sliding window → three-way decision (`none` / `review` / `retrain`) → a `MIN_IMPROVEMENT` (0.005 mAP50) gate on any retrained candidate before registry promotion — argued as a decision-theoretic guard against shipping training-noise-level deltas as if they were improvements.
- **The vision-layer operating point as a chosen tradeoff, not a fixed property**, formalized as a precision/recall curve with the reported 0.858/0.719 pair identified as its F1-argmax, and the `fish_count == 0` rule's total-absence semantics stated explicitly as the boundary of what the downstream system actually checks (H7) — closing the "so what" gap between a vision benchmark number and the safety architecture it feeds.

## Expected Contributions

### Systems/Architecture
- A concrete, code-verifiable instantiation of "deterministic authority over a probabilistic component," generalized as a formal escalate-only monotonicity guarantee enforced by literal code-sharing between production and evaluation paths (not merely a diagram-level claim).
- A demonstrated instance of *evidence-driven* abandonment of LLM-based agentic tool routing — the deterministic router was adopted because a benchmark showed the target-scale LLM fails at reliable tool-calling, not from precaution alone — together with the benchmark's own constant-answer-detection technique, named as a small, transferable evaluation-methodology contribution in its own right (H3).

- An explicit trace from a vision detector's chosen operating point to the one safety rule that consumes its output, disclosing rather than hiding the boundary of what that rule actually checks (H7).
- An asymmetric-cost RAG threshold-selection methodology (hard-negative sweep + explicit cost asymmetry) as a transferable calibration pattern for hallucination-sensitive RAG deployments in high-stakes domains.
- A diagnosed and corrected drift-detection failure mode (equal-width vs. quantile PSI binning under narrow-band confidence outputs) plus a minimum-improvement retraining gate, contributed as a general MLOps pattern independent of the aquaculture domain.
- The cross-cutting claim that this is one design philosophy — not four unrelated engineering choices — consistently applied and independently tested at four architecturally distinct layers within a single deployed system, including one full disclosed negative result, with a fifth, sibling evidence-discipline thread (the vision export/quantization measurement practice) applied to the sensing layer that supplies all four.

### Practice
Actionable design guidelines for practitioners deploying LLMs in safety-critical industrial/agricultural systems:
1. Never let an evaluation harness re-implement business/safety logic that must stay synchronized with production; import the shared module directly.
2. Measure fp32/full-precision export accuracy in isolation, per export target, before attributing any accuracy loss to quantization — export post-processing paths can be the real cause (H6).
3. Before trusting a small (sub-2B) on-device LLM with agentic tool-calling, benchmark its parseable-action rate on realistic scenarios, and check for constant-answer artifacts before trusting an aggregate selection percentage; deterministic routing may dominate at this model scale.
4. For any confidence-style output metric concentrated in a narrow real-valued band, use quantile-edge (not equal-width) binning for PSI-based drift detection.
5. When calibrating RAG similarity thresholds for a high-stakes domain, optimize for asymmetric error cost (fabrication vs. omission), not raw F1 alone.
6. When a detector's output feeds a downstream deterministic rule, trace the rule's actual trigger condition (e.g., total-frame absence vs. per-instance count) against the detector's precision/recall operating point explicitly — do not assume a headline recall number implies proportional safety coverage.
